Review common questions

Good questions

Clarity before commitment.

Is an auction just for distressed properties?

No. Different properties and circumstances can call for different approaches. The right method depends on actual buyer demand, goals, costs and the written rules.

Will my home sell faster or for more?

There is no guarantee. A structured competitive process may be useful in some circumstances, but it cannot create qualified buyers where there is no demand.

What is a reserve price?

Some auction formats have reserve or seller-confirmation conditions; others do not. Rights and obligations depend on the specific signed terms and must be explained before you choose.

Does the seller have to accept an offer?

Asking about options does not require you to sell. Once you sign listing or auction documents, the actual obligations follow those written terms. We won't claim a blanket right to reject bids.

How would Rivet Auctions and Rivet Real Estate work together?

The proposed process begins with a Rivet Auctions agreement. At the same appointment, after the first agreement, the homeowner would review and separately sign a Rivet Real Estate listing agreement for brokerage services. The roles and costs of each business would be explained before any signature. This is a nonproduction concept pending review of the actual agreements.

Can my buyer use an agent?

Buyers can work with their agents under the property's written participation rules. Brokerage compensation is negotiated separately under the applicable signed agreements.

What will it cost?

Fees and any buyer premium must be explained and documented for the property before participants commit. We are not showing a fee schedule in this draft while the financial model is under review.
