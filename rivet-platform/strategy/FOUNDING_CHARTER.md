# Rivet Auctions — Founding Charter & Atlas Decision Record
**Adopted for development:** November 11, 2026  
**Status:** Founder-direction working charter; simulated Atlas review, not a meeting of human employees.  
**Business:** Rivet Auctions; Texas real estate only. Its founder reports holding both a real estate broker license and an auctioneer license. Current status and exact licensed names/numbers must be verified before advertising.

## The decisive insight

Rivet Auctions is not an auction-first sales funnel. It is a **seller-decision and competitive-sale business** in which one professional can bring both real estate brokerage and auctioneering expertise to a property, where appropriate. The fiduciary and agency obligations of brokerage, auction terms, and consumer consent must stay clear; dual qualifications do not dissolve the distinction.

**Positioning:** Broker-led guidance. Auctioneer-led competition when it serves the property. One transparent decision process.

**Primary market:** Austin and Central Texas. Expand only after proof of unit economics and repeatable lawful delivery.

## Vision
To make Rivet Auctions Central Texas's most trusted way to explore competitive real estate sales—where homeowners understand the choices, buyers understand the process, and sellers remain in control.

## Mission
Help property owners understand their options, assess whether a competitive sale fits their goals, and execute the chosen plan with clear terms, accountable professional leadership, and verified buyer interest.

## Brand promise
**A clearer way to sell. Real options. Honest guidance. Competition when it makes sense.**

Operational pledge: We explain the routes, costs, timing, risks, and required agreements; recommend the one we believe is most appropriate; never manufacture bids, urgency, or buyer competition; and respect the homeowner's decision, including not selling.

### Public-facing language
- Headline: **Your property. Your options. A clearer way to sell.**
- Subhead: **Guidance from a Texas real estate broker and auctioneer. We'll help you compare a conventional listing, an as-is sale, and a structured competitive sale—then decide what fits your situation.**
- Primary CTA: **Explore my selling options**
- Secondary CTA: **See how a competitive sale works**
- Buyer CTA: **Ask about a property**
- No unsupported price, timing, guaranteed outcome, or buyer-count claims.

## Core values — observable behaviors
1. **Truth over transaction.** Explain when an auction is unsuitable and why. Document a no-go recommendation.
2. **Homeowner agency.** No pressured commitment, no automatic representation, no lead diversion without permission.
3. **Real competition.** Never invent bidders, offers, demand, reserve status, or urgency; use written, approved auction terms.
4. **Transparent costs and roles.** Disclose brokerage representation, auction terms, premiums if any, and relevant interests before people commit.
5. **Dignity and privacy.** Avoid shame- or fear-based seller copy; protect contact details and circumstances behind staff-only access.
6. **Speed with accountability.** One owner and next action for every lead; tell people what happens next.
7. **Learning with evidence.** Label experiments as experiments; distinguish postcard response from appointments, listings, and closings.

## Who we are for

**Sellers:** Owners comparing ordinary brokerage marketing with a defined competitive sale. Examples: an expired listing, a distinctive asset, a time-sensitive transition, an as-is property, or a seller seeking a transparent sales process. Do not imply all of these are auction-suitable.

**Buyers and agents:** People who want accurate property information, fair written participation terms, timely answers, and clear information on how offers or bids will be reviewed.

**Not our promise:** Higher sale price guaranteed; cash guaranteed; fastest closing guaranteed; bidders guaranteed; auction is right for everyone; a public bid value automatically becomes a verified offer.

## Seller decision model
1. **Listen:** Desired outcome, timeline, property condition, debts/constraints, representation status, sensitivity and authority.
2. **Assess:** Three paths where appropriate: traditional listing, structured competitive/auction sale, or as-is/cash pathway. Include keep-home or no-sale where relevant.
3. **Challenge:** Auction suitability requires evidence of buyer pool, market exposure, reserve/terms feasibility, property eligibility, and net proceeds analysis.
4. **Explain:** Net proceeds ranges, costs, timelines, reserve and contingencies, marketing plan, risks, and who represents whom.
5. **Decide:** Client chooses. Document consent, engagement structure and auction agreement where applicable.
6. **Execute:** Property prep, MLS where appropriate and permitted, buyer verification, announced terms, offers/bids logged, transaction and compliance documentation.
7. **Close and learn:** Settlement, seller outcome, economics, complaint/dispute review, follow-up.

## Simulated Atlas decision meeting (role-based analysis)

**Nathan / Strategy:** This is a homeowner choice company before it is an auction company. Go on building. No-go on any public claim that auctions always outperform listings.

**Morgan / Operations:** A homeowner request is a managed case with an accountable owner, an SLA, one next action and a decision log—not a loose contact form. Auction execution gets its own checklist and human approval gates.

**Marcus / Branding:** Dual broker/auctioneer capability is a meaningful founder distinction, but not a reason to make a seller feel trapped in an auction. The voice is plain, calm, confident and professional. Ditch fine-art remnants.

**Sophia / Sales:** Use two separate pathways (seller and buyer); ask the fewest questions at first and deepen the conversation after consent. A direct-mail response deserves a callback and a source code, not an auto-assigned auction pitch.

**Leila / Research:** The reported additional yellow-postcard response is promising but unverified against a denominator or attribution. Identify which business owns the campaign before recording a Rivet result. Test segments with lead-to-appointment and lead-to-contract conversion, not response rate alone.

**Daniel / Finance:** Spending may be worthwhile, but gate it by unit economics. Quote recurring infrastructure and paid campaign costs. Use a test budget only after baseline funnel, legal approvals and response capacity. Track gross revenue, direct selling costs, cost per qualified lead, cost per appointment, and contribution margin by acquisition source.

**Olivia / Delivery:** Define seller handoff, buyer bid/offer documentation, title exceptions, nonperforming buyers, cancelled auctions and unsold properties before staging a live event.

**Legal/Security challenger:** Do not use a dual-license claim in published materials until the current TREC/TDLR licensed names and license numbers are confirmed. For 2026 TREC IABS, publish the completed current form and deliver it at required conversations. Auction advertising must comply with TDLR license-name/number and any premium/absolute-reserve requirements. Public pages must not expose the team desk or customer-data APIs. Review actual engagement/entity/agency arrangements for each deal.

**Board decision:** **GO** on private development, CRM, marketing experiments and rehearsal. **NO-GO** on public launch, unsupervised bidding, automated offers, or paid campaigns until the readiness gates pass.

### Questions to keep challenging (not blockers to private development)
- Why would this seller choose a competitive sale instead of a normal Rivet listing or an IWCO cash offer?
- What happens if the auction ends below the reserve, attracts no qualified bidders, or produces a failed buyer?
- Could our messaging or dual role create confusion about seller representation, fees, or how bids are verified?
